# Author-grounded manuscript shape

Read before planning or revising a paper, and again when judging whole-paper clarity. This governs selection and organization; house-style's sentence-level integrity rules govern the subsequent copyedit. Explicit user scope and venue requirements take precedence.

## Establish the paper before drafting

Use the author's vault as an index to original papers, not as evidence of writing style by itself. Select two or three accessible originals close in purpose and audience; read their introduction, core explanatory passage, results and discussion, and inspect their section/appendix structure. Prefer the user's chosen examples. Record exact version and source locators. If sources are missing, say so and use a provisional plan rather than claim an author match. An optional author-profile example is `yuan-ji.md` in this directory. Select it only when relevant; record local vault/source paths in the project plan, not shared skills.

Write a compact `manuscript_plan.md` in the project before section drafting:
- Paper type, reader, central question and one-sentence answer; two or three supporting claims.
- Exemplar sources and what transfers: narrative sequence, running example, level of formalism, display selection and placement of technical detail. Do not import a source's scientific mistakes or reproduce its wording.
- A section outline with one purpose per section and a total main-body word target, excluding abstract, references and supplement; separate supplementary budget. Set these from the closest exemplar and venue, not from the largest available draft. Record the counting method and comparable units: typeset two-column pages and double-spaced draft pages are not interchangeable.
- Main-text display allowance and a main/supplement/reproducibility-archive disposition for each result family, proof and inherited section. State why each main-text item is needed. If no closer template exists, a focused methods/comparison paper may start provisionally at 4,000–6,000 main-body words and 4–6 combined tables/figures; these are planning defaults, not scientific limits or targets to fill.

For a revision, preserve an immutable baseline and result lineage; preservation does not require repeating every old paragraph in the new article. Relocate technical material with a recorded destination. Explicitly requested main-text content stays there; resolve genuinely conflicting requirements with the user, but do ordinary editorial selection autonomously. A review or technical monograph can justify more space; do not force all genres into a short-paper template.

## Separate three products

The **main paper** contains the central argument, sufficient setup, selected decisive results and their important qualifications. A running example should do repeated explanatory work. State familiar results briefly with citations; use formal propositions when they advance this paper's argument, with short intuition in the text and longer proofs in the supplement.

The **scientific supplement** contains supporting proofs, generalizations and sensitivity detail needed to assess the claims. Cite related supplemental results together where appropriate; avoid a mandatory main-text paragraph per table. Deliver a separately titled supplement PDF/source when it would otherwise obscure the main-paper length. Essential assumptions and unfavorable findings that change the conclusion remain visible in the main paper.

The **reproducibility archive** retains full grids, audit IDs, checks, numerical tolerances, source hashes and workflow records. These support verification; they do not each require a theorem, table, figure or discussion paragraph in the article. Keep a disposition record for all required evidence, including adverse cases. Use ordinary scientific language in the paper and keep internal anomaly/phase IDs in that record.

## Control scope while writing and reviewing

Every writer receives the same plan and its own section allowance. After assembly, read the paper end-to-end for repetition, notation burden and whether a reader can follow the central argument without the supplement. Compare actual main/supplement word counts and displays with the plan. An overrun above about 10% prompts an editorial pass: combine duplicate explanations, choose informative displays, relocate technical detail, then state any scientifically necessary exception. Keep equations, assumptions and inconvenient results intact; shrinking fonts or removing qualifications is not a length solution.

The critic and grader assess article-scale clarity in addition to paragraph style. Report concrete redundant sections/displays and a proposed disposition. Completeness means the agreed claims are supported in the appropriate artifact, not that every computed cell is printed. Scope comparators to the scientific question and applicable experiment. A diagnostic or prior-sensitivity table need not repeat unrelated methods.

A final style-only pass does not silently delete content. Structural reductions happen in the drafting/editorial pass under this plan, with preserved evidence and relocation records; then house-style checks the selected manuscript for scientific integrity. Scores and formatting passes cannot substitute for reading the whole paper.
